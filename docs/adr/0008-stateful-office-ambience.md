# ADR 0008: Stateful office ambience

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-18

## Context

The office should feel inhabited and expressive while remaining a truthful, read-only view of provider activity. The three examples use different boundaries: [Agent Virtual Office](https://github.com/KbWen/agent-virtual-office/blob/460d71db3b41746748e53e881d7ecd08e1207f60/src/systems/officeLife.js) stages coffee-break scenes and reduces ambient events during a live activity pulse; [Agent Office](https://github.com/Pixel-Process-UG/agent-office/blob/da62eee3bb054d0500b15ace53dd005adcfa512d/src/WelcomeOnboarding.tsx) uses user-configured focus/presence for agents it manages; [Pixel Agents](https://github.com/pixel-agents-hq/pixel-agents/blob/main/webview-ui/src/office/components/ToolOverlay.tsx) displays provider-driven tool/subagent activity in overlays. This dashboard adopts their separation of character animation, office context, and real activity, but not user-invented work states, orchestration, or raw tool-content display.

## Source patterns reviewed

- [Agent Virtual Office `App.jsx`](https://github.com/KbWen/agent-virtual-office/blob/460d71db3b41746748e53e881d7ecd08e1207f60/src/App.jsx) composes the pixel office with a control panel and activity feed. Its [`officeLife.js`](https://github.com/KbWen/agent-virtual-office/blob/460d71db3b41746748e53e881d7ecd08e1207f60/src/systems/officeLife.js) stages a tea/coffee break (one character drinks while others chat); real activity retains priority and ambient frequency drops when a live pulse is present.
- [Agent Office onboarding](https://github.com/Pixel-Process-UG/agent-office/blob/da62eee3bb054d0500b15ace53dd005adcfa512d/src/WelcomeOnboarding.tsx) and its [agent data model](https://github.com/Pixel-Process-UG/agent-office/blob/da62eee3bb054d0500b15ace53dd005adcfa512d/src/data.ts) show focus, presence, and critical-task values are user-configured for managed agents. [`world.ts`](https://github.com/Pixel-Process-UG/agent-office/blob/da62eee3bb054d0500b15ace53dd005adcfa512d/src/world.ts) maps idle, active, meeting, paused, and blocked states to sprites.
- [Pixel Agents `OfficeCanvas.tsx`](https://github.com/pixel-agents-hq/pixel-agents/blob/main/webview-ui/src/office/components/OfficeCanvas.tsx) renders agent state in the scene; [`ToolOverlay.tsx`](https://github.com/pixel-agents-hq/pixel-agents/blob/main/webview-ui/src/office/components/ToolOverlay.tsx) exposes subtask titles on hover and live tool activity on selection. This informs the legible overlay/feed pattern, not permission to display raw tool details here.

These are source-level implementation patterns, not proof that our providers expose the same events or that fictional scenes are real activity.

## Decision

- Keep a **factual layer** and a **fictional office layer** distinct. Provider identity, relationships, state, freshness, titles, and lifecycle remain authoritative and visibly available. Only observed provider transitions enter the global recent-activity feed; fictional scenes, quips, props, and setup actions never do.
- Use distinct, accessible status cues: varied desk-centered work poses/props for working without task-specific claims; a visible wait cue; attentive approval/blocked cue; neutral issue cue for failure; brief completion flourish followed by rest; neutral/solo-fiction pose for unknown. Working characters do not roam away from their desk. Preserve readable factual state/freshness labels and reduced-motion support.
- Use brief authored decorative dialogue in an animated speech bubble. Cycle authored lines per character and state without repeats until that line bank is exhausted; a bubble gets about 15–30 seconds between appearances and remains long enough to read (generic 200 wpm estimate, bounded to 3–8 seconds). The bubble pops/fades; reduced motion keeps it static for the reading window. A distinct visual style plus an accessible label identifies it as fictional without repeated visible disclaimer text; never append a duplicate factual status line. Stable voice flavors (curious, dry-witted, earnest, calm, upbeat) are decorative, not provider personality/role/capability. Playful lines may appear in any state but cannot mock or blame the user/task, and never obscure an actionable approval/error cue.
- Office vignettes are fictional and may involve characters across provider-reported clusters; they do not assert collaboration. Fresh, connected agents with an explicit provider `idle` state may join group scenes; expressly ended characters remain visible by default and may join only with idle or ended characters. Unknown/stale/disconnected characters receive lively solo fiction only; they are never presented as working/idle or used as social participants. If a participant transitions to working, dismiss the whole group scene and restore desk-centered presentation immediately. Vignettes recur about every 10–30 seconds while eligible participants are present and shuffle without repeating a template until the bank is exhausted. The optional Show ended control can hide ended characters.
- **Quiet-cluster fiction** applies when no fresh agent in that cluster is Working, Waiting, or Failed, including when the source is disconnected or agents are Unknown. This is a fictional office atmosphere, not a normalized “session idle” state or an inferred end. For this visual mode, dim the room and hide title/motifs while retaining characters, factual badges, session identity, and props. Do not show a “Session ended” marker. On a fresh Working/Waiting/Failed signal, restore normal light/title/motifs immediately; real attention-needed state is never delayed or hidden. Quiet-cluster effects use the same 10–30-second, nonrepeating authored ambience cycle. The `Show ended` default includes explicitly ended characters but does not change provider state.
- Unknown or stale agents can take solo fictional actions while preserving their Unknown/Stale/disconnected badges. Their solo motion/dialogue is never a group scene or evidence of present work. Disconnected session quiet ambience is likewise presentation only.
- A session returning to activity restores its previous title/motifs and normal lighting; props remain until visibly tidied or the instance closes.
- **Abandoned clusters** remain a separate factual-lifecycle presentation: only explicit provider session/thread closure triggers an end marker/time; Codex `thread/closed` runtime unload does not qualify. Keep ended characters visible by default as off-duty; the existing Show ended control can hide them. Ended characters may walk gently to the common area and join scenes with idle/ended characters, without changing their Ended state. Spooky props/lighting must remain fiction and must not relabel the factual session status.
- The authored idle-scene bank includes coffee/tea, stretching/walking, plant care, desk tidying, generic reading, light antics, and friendly group chat. Separate controls for office vignettes and dialogue are on by default; disabling either leaves factual status and feed intact.
- Use deterministic local scene motifs sourced only from the provider-supplied session title, never prompt/transcript/tool content. The catalog has work-type and technical-area axes (implementation, debugging, refactoring, review, testing, docs, design, research, planning, performance, accessibility, security, data/schema, APIs, dependencies, CI/build, release, migration, cleanup, configuration; frontend, backend, storage, APIs, mobile, desktop, cloud/infra, CLI/tooling, ML/AI, graphics/games). Show at most four high-confidence motifs per session cluster (up to two from each axis); use neutral decor when uncertain. Motifs decorate the session cluster, not agent identity. A provider title may contain sensitive text: show it in the roster by default with global and per-session hide controls. Hiding title text does not hide its derived motif; explain this in the control. When a session ends, remove its title and motif.
- Office props persist for the open canvas lifetime. Props are removed only through visible cleanup or when the office closes. Any currently idle agent may tidy a prop across clusters. A newly arriving agent has one exception: if its own reused desk is dirty, show a one-time, separately labeled “Setting up desk” scene; keep its provider status unchanged and do not add the scene to the activity feed.
- A cluster becomes **abandoned** only after explicit provider-reported session/thread closure with no agents remaining. An explicit agent-end signal exits that character; ordinary turn completion/failure, idle, archive, stale, or disconnected state alone does not trigger departure or haunting. An empty ended cluster keeps a neutral “Session ended” marker/time and its props, but loses its title/motif. It is softly dimmed and may show whimsical-spooky ambience: brief ghost, spider, screen-flicker-without-error-cue, or chair movement; a web persists as a prop until tidied. A returning session restores its identity/title/motif and normal lighting. A different session may reuse the physical space, but receives a new cluster identity and context rather than inheriting the old session.
- User appearance overrides are stored locally by provider identity when stable, otherwise by session. A deterministic provider-identity look is the default. Users may change sprite/outfit, palette, accessory/desk prop, and local nickname; nickname is prominent while provider name/role remain available as factual secondary identity. Do not add user-authored role/focus/task fields.

## Decision update (2026-10-18)

The office is one warm, lived-in pixel-art studio with distinct, seeded decorative layouts and named physical [Office zones](../../GLOSSARY.md): workstation areas, dividers, one office-wide multipurpose common table and adjacent coffee station, a plant corner, bookshelf, sunny window, wall art, seating, and bins. The table and coffee station are shared physical zones, not copies inside each provider-established session cluster; agents may travel from their cluster-local desks to these global zones and return. A physical zone is not a session cluster; layout and furnishings never imply provider role, team, or shared workspace. Keep the existing character sprite style. Use original, in-repository pixel-grid SVG assets for furniture, props, pests, and action objects; do not use lettering, logos, Unicode glyphs, or visible text labels as substitutes for physical objects. Factual labels and authored dialogue remain text under the existing accessibility/fact-fiction rules.

Action vignettes are complete, location-consistent visual sequences, not labels: eligible characters walk along defined routes, visibly perform an action, carry or leave its prop, and return to their desk/zone. Fresh connected idle and explicitly ended characters may use shared facilities and the previously allowed group scenes. Unknown/Stale/Disconnected characters may take solo trips while retaining their factual badges but never participate in group interactions. Working/Waiting/Failed characters remain desk-centered; a factual state change immediately cancels incompatible fiction. Reading happens seated at the common table with a visible book asset. Coffee visits the adjacent station, shows brewing and carrying a mug back to the character's desk; the mug persists until visible cleanup. A pixel-art spider can cross a desk and leave a web that remains until an eligible character returns to use that desk and visibly cleans it. A paper-hat vignette visibly changes the sprite temporarily and then restores it. Other existing scenarios follow the same action/location/prop grammar.

Dialogue and ambience use one staggered scheduler to avoid simultaneous bubbles; demo timing is seeded for repeatable frames, while the factual event feed remains provider-ordered. Physical decorations, routes, and actions are presentation state only and never change provider state, identity, sorting, navigation, or feed entries.

Character badges show high-contrast provider state only; provider remains identified on the session, and provider/source/freshness details remain available in the agent details panel. Hiding a provider title removes the title text and any placeholder; title-derived motifs remain visible per the existing decision, and the control explains that behavior. The local scenario catalog must expose named frames for each scene/action family, including the paper-hat appearance change, through the production office path.

### Follow-up refinement — collision-free furnishings and legible motion (2026-10-18)

Use a small set of hand-authored, collision-tested workstation layouts selected deterministically per session cluster; do not jitter furniture or place each prop independently. Each desk has one anchored chair, dividers and wall art occupy reserved non-overlapping slots, and plants live in the single plant corner. The shared table has four fixed seats and accepts groups only up to available seating; the adjacent coffee station is single-user. The common area has one designated waste/recycling station rather than duplicate loose bins. Props may overlap only when their attachment is intentional (for example, a book on the table, a web on a desk, or an agent in a seat). Character and ghost routes use walkable floor/aisle anchors and must not pass through walls or furniture. A screen-flicker effect changes an existing desk monitor only; it never creates another screen. A ghost is a roaming fictional actor, not a static boxed corner icon. The external scenario player must make named frames easy to browse and optionally play through; scenario text remains outside the office renderer.

## Provider-signal interpretation

An office departure requires a genuine provider-reported agent/session end. In particular, Codex app-server `thread/closed` is runtime unload/subscription closure, not proof that the conversation ended; see [ADR 0007's protocol clarification](./0007-provider-consent-and-discovery.md#protocol-clarification-2026-10-18). It produces an unmonitored/unknown observation and must not trigger an abandoned cluster. The world model can render explicitly ended clusters when a supported provider reports them; absence of that capability is not filled with invented exits.

## Consequences

- UI tests must assert that fiction never changes normalized provider state, freshness, sorting, navigation, or the factual event feed.
- Event-driven transitions must cancel incompatible scenes immediately, without delaying a provider-reported working/approval/error state.
- Scene props and dialogue are presentation state, not session history or task artifacts; clear them when the canvas closes. Local identity preferences are separate and may persist.
- Do not reuse source-project assets or copy their code as part of adopting these patterns; review licenses separately.
